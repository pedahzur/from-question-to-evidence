# Notes: WS2 Foundation Package

## Binding requirements

- Chapter target: approximately 2,500–3,500 words when drafted.
- Required claim: auditability exposes situated judgment to criticism but does not make judgment neutral or complete.
- Coverage refers to the declared evidence design, not the phenomenon or world.
- The researcher must appear as a record-producing, category-producing, and relationship-bearing actor.
- The chapter must address emergent inquiry, interpretive work, participatory authority, feminist and decolonial methods, archival power, unequal visibility, and co-produced evidence.
- No guided AI workflow may precede the AI pre-flight gate.
- The gate must decide authorization, data classification, necessity, model access, rights, validation, logging, and escalation.

## Research notes

### Chapter architecture

- The governing claim is that recording position, relationships, categories, institutions, and access makes judgment reviewable without making it neutral or complete.
- The chapter needs seven movements: synthetic opening pressure; situated knowledge; the researcher inside the evidence chain; distributed authority; emergent design; limits of auditability; and the saved epistemic-position statement.
- The chapter artifact must change downstream decisions. A generic autobiographical or identity paragraph does not pass.

### Source verification

- Foundational and methods sources were verified against JSTOR, SAGE, Taylor and Francis, Cambridge Core, Routledge, Bloomsbury, DOI, and publisher records.
- The bounded core queue assigns one argumentative load to each work. Metadata verification is complete; page-level claim verification remains a drafting requirement.
- Priority 1 sources cover situated knowledge, dialogical fieldwork, forms of reflexivity, active reflexive practice, decolonial authority, damage-centered research, archival silence and power, and method-specific transparency.
- Counterweights cover performative positionality statements, the costs of compulsory visibility, colonial archival practice, and method-specific quality criteria.
- NIST AI 600-1, the Association of Internet Researchers' Ethics 3.0 guidance, and UNESCO's research guidance support stable pre-flight obligations. Provider settings and policies remain dated companion material.

### AI gate design

- The gate decides purpose and necessity, authority, material classification, processing environment, minimization, risk, validation, logging, escalation, approval, and closeout.
- Unresolved authority, data classification, provider conditions, validation, or disproportionate risk stops the affected use.
- Participant-produced, confidential, restricted, and pseudonymized material remains barred from external AI systems by default.
- Approval expires when the task, material, system, authority, or release audience changes.
