## Objective

Transform the current strong evidence-building manuscript into a coherent, complete, tested, and accessible qualitative-methods textbook that fulfills its promise to move from question to corpus, analysis, claim, writing, responsible sharing, and maintenance.

The plan preserves the book's strongest contribution: consequential research decisions become durable, inspectable artifacts, and AI remains a bounded proposal and transformation layer rather than a source of scholarly authority.

## Definition of Done

The manuscript is ready for a 1.0 release only when all of the following are true:

1. The title, subtitle, description, opening, table of contents, and conclusion describe the same complete product.
2. One master lifecycle connects question, evidence design, collection, preparation, analysis, claim, writing, release, and maintenance.
3. The book states its epistemological commitments, limits, and adaptation paths before the first procedure.
4. AI integrity appears before the first guided AI workflow.
5. A complete composite oral-history case demonstrates how the method changes when evidence is co-produced.
6. Collection and preparation, analysis and claim testing, and writing and responsible release exist as full modules.
7. Every core module has an annotated completed artifact, blank resource, integrity gate, and observable completion criterion.
8. Novice and experienced readers have tested representative pathways, and the manuscript records revisions made from their use.
9. General methodological claims rest on an appropriate, verified scholarly evidence base.
10. HTML, DOCX, PDF, and Markdown pass content, citation, rights, accessibility, link, and visual checks.
11. The public release has a version, manifest, change log, correction route, preservation copy, and explicit retirement or update policy.

## Master Lifecycle and Artifact Chain

All domain-specific sequences must map to this single lifecycle:

| Lifecycle stage | Governing question | Minimum durable artifact | Human gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Position | What kind of knowledge is sought, by whom, and under which commitments? | Epistemic and positionality statement | Approve the claim type and authority structure |
| 2. Frame | What is the bounded or provisionally bounded inquiry? | Question frame | Approve scope, exclusions, and answer form |
| 3. Design evidence | What concepts, cases, actors, and source families could bear on the question? | Concept grid and source-family map | Approve categories and rival routes |
| 4. Discover | How will candidate evidence be found without confusing retrieval with coverage? | Search and discovery log | Approve search changes and candidate promotion rules |
| 5. Collect | What may be gathered, from whom, and under which permissions? | Collection protocol and permission register | Approve access, consent, sampling, and collection |
| 6. Prepare | Which transformations make evidence usable, and how is lineage preserved? | Transformation and provenance log | Approve corrected derivatives and corpus admission |
| 7. Analyze | Which comparisons, codes, sequences, or interpretations answer the question? | Analytic memos, matrices, and codebook | Approve analytical changes and unresolved disagreement |
| 8. Test claims | What supports, qualifies, contradicts, or fails to assess each claim? | Claim-dependency and disconfirmation register | Approve load-bearing claims and limits |
| 9. Express | How will the argument preserve evidence, attribution, uncertainty, and dissent? | Versioned manuscript and change record | Approve prose, citations, figures, and disclosures |
| 10. Release and maintain | What may be shared, withheld, corrected, updated, preserved, or retired? | Release manifest and maintenance policy | Approve public release and later changes |

The evidence-map, literature, historical, event-database, and PKM sequences become views over this lifecycle. They no longer compete as independent architectures.

**Priority:** Blocker

- **Output:** a lawful, ethical, documented, analysis-ready corpus with an auditable handoff.

### Chapter tasks

#### 4.1 Collection as Evidence

- Distinguish source discovery, evidence collection, encounter production, and corpus admission.
- Define the collection protocol and source/participant authority.
- State what changes across documentary, interview, observational, visual, audio, and born-digital evidence.

#### 4.2 Permission, Access, Sampling, and Field Relations

- Cover ethical review, consent, data agreements, archive conditions, gatekeepers, community rules, and lawful basis.
- Distinguish theoretical, purposive, snowball, maximum-variation, negative-case, and source-frame sampling at the level necessary for the book's method.
- Show how access routes shape the corpus.

#### 4.3 Capture Encounters, Documents, and Media

- Cover interview and observation records, field notes, document capture, screenshots, images, audio, video, and web material.
- Specify original, derivative, metadata, locator, and contextual memo requirements.
- Separate public availability from permission to aggregate or republish.

#### 4.4 Record Context, Provenance, and Researcher Position

- Extend provenance to intent, relationship, coercion, silence, institutional process, and researcher involvement.
- Record participant terms, changing consent, field position, translation authority, and contested labels.

#### 4.5 Transcribe, Translate, Normalize, and Prepare

- Treat OCR, transcription, translation, segmentation, anonymization, pseudonymization, normalization, coding preparation, and format conversion as declared transformations.
- Define verification samples and error categories appropriate to each operation.
- Preserve original and corrected forms.

#### 4.6 Secure, Store, Preserve, Withdraw, and Delete

- Cover sensitivity classes, access control, encryption policy at the level of principle, backups, retention, withdrawal, deletion obligations, file identity, and preservation formats.
- Separate local/private research records from public release objects.

#### 4.7 Audit and Hand Off the Corpus

- Produce a corpus manifest, permissions matrix, transformation log, restriction record, quality report, and unresolved-items queue.
- Define readiness for analysis and conditions that reopen collection or preparation.

## WS5 — Analysis and Claim-Testing Module

**Priority:** Blocker
**Estimated effort:** 15–22 focused days
**Dependencies:** G4
**Accountable:** Ami
**Drafting, examples, and tests:** Codex

### Module contract

- **Target length:** 14,000–18,000 words before references.
- **Output:** a documented path from corpus to interpretation and from interpretation to bounded claim.
- **Principle:** analytical method follows question, evidence, and claim type; no software taxonomy organizes the module.

### Chapter tasks

#### 5.1 From Corpus to Analytic Design

- Match descriptive, interpretive, comparative, and causal questions to evidence and analytical operations.
- Define unit of analysis, case, observation, episode, sequence, text span, and claim.
- State what the corpus cannot support.

#### 5.2 Close Reading, Coding, and Memoing

- Explain deductive, inductive, abductive, and hybrid coding.
- Treat codebooks as versioned arguments, not clerical labels.
- Preserve source voice, researcher memo, uncertainty, and rejected categories.

#### 5.3 Compare Cases, Contexts, and Sequences

- Cover within-case and cross-case comparison, matrices, typologies, temporal bracketing, and contextual equivalence.
- Prevent empty-cell and missing-data states from collapsing into one category.

#### 5.4 Narrative, Discourse, and Meaning

- Address sequence, voice, genre, framing, category use, institutional language, and counter-narrative.
- Preserve the difference between what was said, how it was said, and what the researcher infers.

#### 5.5 Process Tracing, Rival Explanations, and Negative Cases

- Define causal-process observations, sequence, mechanism, alternative explanations, and diagnostic evidence.
- Integrate disconfirmation throughout analysis rather than in a final caveat.
- Move Bayesian updating from the event-database chapter into an advanced method after the qualitative logic is established.

#### 5.6 Qualitative Counting and Visualization

- Explain when counts, distributions, timelines, networks, and maps clarify a corpus.
- Show why frequency is not automatically importance, prevalence, or causal weight.
- Preserve the denominator, source frame, and missingness model.

#### 5.7 Build and Audit Claims

- Link claims to observations, analytic memos, rival evidence, and uncertainty.
- Classify support, partial support, contradiction, dependence, and non-assessability.
- Define the smallest revision the evidence supports.

### Resources

- analytic-design memo;
- codebook and memo register;
- comparison matrix;
- sequence/process-tracing table;
- negative-case and rival-explanation register;
- qualitative-counting denominator sheet;
- claim-dependency and audit register.

### Acceptance gate G5 — Claim readiness

- Every worked claim can be traced to corpus objects and analytical decisions.
- The module demonstrates at least four distinct qualitative analytical traditions without collapsing them into coding.
- Counterevidence appears before final conclusions.
- Counts and visualizations state their source frame and denominator.
- AI may propose codes or patterns but cannot ratify interpretation or causal importance.

Each core chapter must contain or link to:

4. the methodological decision and its alternatives;
5. a worked fragment from the running case;
6. a second contrasting case where transfer matters;

9. a task-specific integrity checkpoint;

12. citations for every external methodological or empirical claim;
13. accessibility-compliant tables, figures, and headings;

The eight-part page pattern may flex. A chapter should not retain a section that adds only repetition.
