# Literature as Evidence Module Design

**Date:** 2026-07-13  
**Status:** Approved concept; written specification awaiting review  
**Project:** *From Question to Evidence: A Living Field Guide for Qualitative Research*

## Purpose

The second exemplary module will guide researchers from an evidence map to a defensible literature synthesis. It will treat scholarly literature as evidence about concepts, arguments, methods, findings, and scholarly lineages rather than as a pile of publications to summarize.

The module will test whether the page pattern established by *From Question to Evidence Map* can support a substantially different research task while serving novice and experienced researchers on the same pages.

## Central Claim

A literature review becomes analytically useful when the researcher can explain how works were discovered, why they entered the review, what evidentiary role each work plays, where claims converge or conflict, and what the search failed to recover.

AI may assist with vocabulary, comparison, classification, and audit. It may not determine source authority, supply unverified citations, substitute summaries for reading, or erase the researcher's responsibility for synthesis.

## Scope

The module will be approximately 8,000–10,000 words across an overview and six stage pages. It will address literature reviews that support empirical qualitative and mixed-method research. It will not attempt to provide a full systematic-review protocol, a meta-analysis workflow, or a survey of every available discovery and reference-management product.

Product-specific material will appear only in dated tool cards or brief examples. The methodological prose must remain useful when individual platforms change.

## Reader Outcomes

After completing the module, a reader should be able to:

1. State the purpose, boundaries, and review question.
2. Build a concept and vocabulary map that preserves contested meanings.
3. Run and document iterative discovery across database search, citation chaining, and targeted browsing.
4. Evaluate a work's relevance, authority, provenance, and role in the developing argument.
5. Annotate and compare works without reducing the review to serial summaries.
6. Produce a synthesis matrix, synthesis memo, search-and-AI audit log, and reasoned stopping rule.
7. Explain what remains missing and what evidence would reopen the review.

## Module Architecture

### Overview: Literature as Evidence

The overview will distinguish a literature review from a bibliography, reading list, and automated summary. It will introduce the module's connected artifacts and show how the review inherits boundaries and source families from the evidence map.

### Stage 1: Define the Review

The reader will identify the review's function: conceptual clarification, theory building, empirical orientation, methodological comparison, gap identification, or a justified combination. The saved artifact will be a review protocol with inclusion logic, exclusions, date, version, and anticipated answer.

### Stage 2: Map Concepts and Vocabulary

The reader will identify core concepts, rival definitions, historical terms, disciplinary synonyms, translations, acronyms, and adjacent terms. The stage will emphasize that vocabulary expansion changes the visible literature. The saved artifact will be a concept-and-vocabulary map.

### Stage 3: Discover Through Multiple Routes

The reader will combine structured database searching, backward and forward citation chaining, author and institution tracing, handbook or review-article orientation, and targeted web discovery. Search routes will be logged separately so that repeated dependence on one index becomes visible. The saved artifact will be a literature search log.

### Stage 4: Evaluate Sources and Claims

The reader will assess relevance to the review question, publication context, source type, methods, evidence base, citation lineage, limitations, and intended evidentiary role. Citation counts and venue reputation will be treated as signals rather than verdicts. The saved artifact will be a source-evaluation register.

### Stage 5: Read, Annotate, and Compare

The reader will annotate claims, concepts, evidence, methods, limitations, and relations to other works. Notes will distinguish the author's claim from the reviewer's interpretation. The stage will move from document-level notes to a cross-source comparison matrix. The saved artifact will be a synthesis matrix.

### Stage 6: Synthesize, Audit, and Stop

The reader will write by pattern, dispute, lineage, method, or explanatory problem rather than one source at a time. The reader will audit missing perspectives, overrepresented venues, circular citation, inaccessible material, language exclusions, and AI-mediated transformations. The saved artifacts will be a synthesis memo and stopping rule.

## Repeated Page Pattern

Every stage page will use the approved sequence:

1. **Orientation** — the decision the stage supports.
2. **Learn** — stable methodological guidance.
3. **Worked Example** — the decision applied to a running case.
4. **Try It** — an exercise on the reader's own project.
5. **Guided AI Workflow** — bounded AI assistance with verification instructions.
6. **Integrity Checkpoint** — provenance, bias, privacy, copyright, and uncertainty checks.
7. **Save the Artifact** — named output, required fields, and versioning guidance.
8. **Advanced Practice** — complications and alternatives for experienced researchers.

## Worked Examples

The primary example will continue the terrorism-database inquiry. It will show how apparently empirical disagreements depend on definitions, event taxonomies, source systems, coding practices, and citation lineages.

A secondary example will follow an archival study of institutional change. It will demonstrate historical vocabulary, disciplinary boundary crossing, uneven digitization, citation chaining from an archival finding, and the difference between absence from an index and absence from the literature.

The second example will remain compact and recur only where it reveals a limitation of the primary example. It will not become a second full narrative that doubles the module's length.

## Artifacts and Templates

The module will add five reusable resources:

1. Literature Review Protocol (`.docx`)
2. Concept and Vocabulary Map (`.xlsx`)
3. Literature Search and AI Audit Log (`.xlsx`)
4. Source Evaluation Register (`.xlsx`)
5. Synthesis Matrix and Stopping Rule (`.xlsx`)

The templates will share stable identifiers and version fields. Each stage page will link directly to the corresponding editable resource in the website profile. The manuscript profile will describe the resource without embedding site-only download cards.

## AI Workflows

AI will have four bounded roles:

- **Generate:** propose synonyms, translations, rival concepts, and missing search routes.
- **Transform:** place researcher-supplied notes into a provisional comparison structure.
- **Compare:** identify agreements, conflicts, uncited dependencies, and empty matrix cells.
- **Audit:** challenge disciplinary, linguistic, geographical, and methodological coverage.

Every workflow will specify permitted input, prohibited input, expected output, verification steps, and the audit record to save. No workflow will ask a model to fabricate citations, judge quality from an abstract alone, or summarize unread sources as if they had been reviewed.

## Evidence and Citation Standard

Methodological claims will be supported by current primary standards or authoritative scholarly methods literature. Legacy citations will be treated as leads until verified. Product documentation may support dated tool cards but will not carry methodological claims.

Direct quotations from legacy drafts will not be reused without confirming authorship and rights. Useful legacy passages will normally be rewritten from the underlying idea and recorded in the editorial audit.

## Integration with the Existing Book

The module overview and six stages will be inserted after the evidence-map module and before the current AI-integrity chapter. The existing *Beyond the Pilot* page will be revised so that it describes the completed two-module book and identifies collection as the next proposed module.

The site navigation, manuscript configuration, automated content checks, citation tests, rendered-site tests, and deliverable tests will be updated to include the new pages and templates.

## Verification and Acceptance Criteria

The module is complete when:

1. All seven pages render in both site and manuscript profiles.
2. Every stage contains the eight approved page sections in order.
3. The five templates open successfully and contain the specified fields.
4. Site download links resolve to the packaged templates.
5. All citations resolve against the bibliography and factual claims are logged for review.
6. Automated tests pass, including new structural and template-contract checks.
7. The rendered site is inspected at desktop and narrow widths.
8. The generated DOCX and PDF contain the complete two-module manuscript.
9. The updated sharing package includes the manuscript, site preview, source archive, editorial note, and verification report.

## Non-Goals

This implementation will not add user accounts, a database-backed editorial workflow, community commenting, live AI calls, automated searches of subscription databases, a Hebrew edition, or the later collection and analysis modules.

## Editorial Risks and Controls

- **Scope inflation:** Keep the module to six decisions and move tool detail to cards.
- **Serial-summary structure:** Require every stage and artifact to operate across sources.
- **AI overreach:** Pair every AI action with source-level verification and an audit record.
- **Disciplinary narrowness:** Use the archival secondary example and coverage audit.
- **Stale legacy guidance:** Verify methodological claims and exclude obsolete interface instructions.
- **Template burden:** Include only fields required by the prose and test them automatically.
