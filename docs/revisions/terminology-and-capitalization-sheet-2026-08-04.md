# Terminology and Capitalization Sheet

## Authority and use

This sheet governs new drafting, structural revision, resources, navigation, and release documentation for *From Question to Evidence*. It standardizes project language without silently changing quotations, archival titles, published source terminology, or exact schema fields. When a legacy chapter uses a competing sequence, the revision must crosswalk that sequence to the ten-stage research lifecycle rather than rename it without explanation.

## Architecture

| Canonical term | Definition and use | Avoid or distinguish from |
|---|---|---|
| **research lifecycle** | The book-level architecture: Position, Frame, Design evidence, Discover, Collect, Prepare, Analyze, Test claims, Express, Release and maintain. It is ordered but recursive. Use `master lifecycle` only in internal governance records when the hierarchy among sequences must be explicit. | Do not call every domain sequence a lifecycle, cycle, or architecture. |
| **lifecycle stage** | One functional transition in the research lifecycle. A stage names work and its human gate; it does not dictate one chapter per stage. | Do not use `step` when iteration or reopening is possible. |
| **pathway** | A shorter, domain-specific sequence that implements part of the research lifecycle, such as the seven-stage evidence-map pathway or six-stage literature pathway. Every pathway must have a lifecycle crosswalk. | Replace `seven-stage architecture`, `PKM-AI lifecycle`, and other rival book-level labels. |
| **Part** | One of the six top-level divisions of the book. Capitalize when followed by its formal number or title. | A Part is not a release module. |
| **module** | A coherent teaching sequence within a Part that can be tested or released as a unit. Use lowercase generically and title case only in a formal named label. | A module does not supersede the six-part architecture. |
| **chapter** | A numbered unit with one principal competence, artifact, or claim. Use lowercase generically and capitalize in formal references such as `Chapter 12`. | Do not preserve a chapter solely to document a tool or release. |

## Inquiry and evidence design

| Canonical term | Definition and use | Avoid or distinguish from |
|---|---|---|
| **epistemic-position statement** | A dated account of the knowledge sought, the researcher's position and authority, the intended claim types, and the method's limits. | It is not a generic reflexivity disclaimer. |
| **question frame** | A versioned statement of the inquiry, intended answer form, provisional scope, exclusions, and conditions that may reopen the frame. | Broader than a one-sentence research question. |
| **question dimension** | A component of the question that must be defined, compared, observed, or explained and can therefore guide evidence design. | Not a keyword or database field, although it may generate both. |
| **boundary axis** | A category used to bound the inquiry, such as time, place, population, language, institution, source family, or claim type. | Not every boundary is an exclusion; some remain provisional. |
| **concept-and-context grid** | The artifact that records concepts, meanings, rival terms, contextual dimensions, operational consequences, and revisions. | Use the full hyphenated name; avoid alternating with `concept grid` in formal instructions. |
| **source** | A document, object, observation, testimony, image, recording, dataset, or born-digital trace that may bear on an inquiry. Whether it functions as evidence depends on the question and claim. | A source is not evidence by file type alone. |
| **source family** | A group of sources produced through a related institutional, social, technical, or communicative process. Production logic, dependence, incentives, and silences define the family. | Not a file format, repository, search result category, or convenient topic group. Use `file type`, `repository`, or `search route` when that is what is meant. |
| **evidence** | Material used in a documented relation to a question, interpretation, or claim. The relation, provenance, and limits must remain inspectable. | Avoid treating `evidence` as a synonym for all collected material. |
| **evidence map** | A versioned system linking the question frame, concept-and-context grid, source-family inventory, search log, evidence register, gap analysis, and stopping rule. A working map changes; a frozen map closes a declared collection phase. | It is neither a search-results list nor a claim of exhaustive knowledge. |
| **coverage** | The degree to which the assembled material addresses the declared dimensions, boundaries, source families, and rival routes in the evidence design. | Never use coverage to imply that the corpus represents the phenomenon or world in full. |
| **gap** | A documented absence, weakness, dependence, contradiction, or access limit relative to the declared evidence design. | Do not equate a search failure with absence in the world. |
| **stopping rule** | A dated argument for ending active work in a defined phase, including adequacy conditions, known limits, unresolved leads, and triggers for reopening. | Not a claim that nothing more exists or that interpretation is complete. |

## Records, states, and transformations

| Canonical term | Definition and use | Avoid or distinguish from |
|---|---|---|
| **artifact** | A durable, inspectable output that records a consequential research decision, transformation, comparison, or gate. | Not every saved file is an artifact. |
| **record** | An identified and documented representation of a source, encounter, observation, or derived object, with enough provenance to track its origin and state. | A record is not automatically verified or admitted to a corpus. |
| **candidate record** | A discovered or proposed record awaiting required identity, provenance, content, rights, and relevance checks. `Candidate` may be used alone only when the object type is already clear. | Never cite or analyze a candidate as if it were accepted evidence. |
| **verified record** | A candidate whose named, method-appropriate checks have been completed against the source, encounter record, or authorized reference. For participant-produced and interpretive material, checks may establish authorization, identity or pseudonym status, provenance, transcript fidelity, or the integrity of the documented process. Verification confirms only the checked properties; it does not establish that a recollection, testimony, observation, or interpretation is true. | Distinguish `verified` from `validated`, which concerns performance against a benchmark, sample, or use condition. |
| **accepted record** | A verified record admitted to the working corpus under declared inclusion, ethical, legal, and analytical criteria. | Acceptance does not authorize public release. |
| **published record** | An accepted record approved for public or otherwise designated release after rights, confidentiality, harm, and disclosure review. | Publication is a separate state, not the default consequence of acceptance. |
| **corpus** | The versioned set of accepted records assembled for a declared research phase or analytical purpose. | Avoid using `archive`, `database`, and `corpus` interchangeably. |
| **provenance** | The documented origin, custody, retrieval route, identifiers, relationships, and transformation history of a source or record. | A URL alone is not complete provenance. |
| **transformation** | A declared change that produces a derivative, such as OCR, transcription, translation, normalization, extraction, redaction, coding, or aggregation. Preserve the prior object and the transformation record. | Do not overwrite a source with a corrected or normalized derivative. |
| **transformation log** | The versioned record of what changed, why, when, by whom or what system, under which settings, and with what validation. | Distinct from a general research diary. |
| **chain of custody** | The record of possession, access, storage, transfer, and integrity controls for material whose authenticity, confidentiality, or legal status requires such tracking. | Use `provenance` for the broader origin and transformation history. |

## Analysis, claims, and accountability

| Canonical term | Definition and use | Avoid or distinguish from |
|---|---|---|
| **analysis** | The documented work of interpreting, coding, comparing, sequencing, counting, or otherwise relating corpus material to the question. | Retrieval, summary, and extraction are preparation unless they answer an analytical question. |
| **analytic memo** | A versioned record of an interpretation, comparison, code change, rival explanation, negative case, or uncertainty and its supporting material. | Not a polished section of manuscript prose. |
| **claim** | A bounded proposition the project asks readers to accept, accompanied by its support, scope, qualifications, contradictions, and unresolved uncertainty. | A source statement, extracted fact, or model output is not automatically a claim of the researcher. |
| **evidence chain** | The linked sequence from source or encounter through records, transformations, observations, interpretations, and claims. | Not proof of neutrality or certainty. |
| **claim-dependency register** | The artifact that links each load-bearing claim to supporting, qualifying, contradicting, and unavailable evidence, plus unresolved verification work. | Broader than a citation list. |
| **audit** | A bounded, documented examination of whether declared rules, states, links, rights, transformations, or claims are present and defensible. Name the object and criteria of every audit. | Avoid `audit` as a vague synonym for review, reflection, or rereading. |
| **audit trail** | The accumulated records that permit reconstruction of decisions and changes. | An audit trail enables criticism; it does not make judgment neutral. |
| **human gate** | A named decision point at which an authorized person approves, rejects, revises, escalates, or abstains before the workflow changes state. | Human presence without authority or recorded action is not a gate. |
| **integrity gate** | A human gate focused on authorization, rights, privacy, safety, provenance, verification, disclosure, or research responsibility. Use `integrity checkpoint` for a teaching prompt that prepares the decision but does not itself authorize transition. | Do not use `human-in-the-loop` as a substitute for naming the decision and authority. |

## AI, publication, and maintenance

| Canonical term | Definition and use | Avoid or distinguish from |
|---|---|---|
| **generative AI** | Systems that generate or transform text, images, audio, code, or structured data. Spell out on first substantive use when readers may need the definition; use `AI` thereafter. | Avoid implying that all computational methods are generative AI. |
| **AI-assisted** | The preferred adjective for a bounded task or workflow in which an AI system proposes, retrieves, transforms, compares, or checks under declared controls. | Avoid `AI-powered`, `smart`, or `autonomous` when a researcher retains authority. |
| **AI-augmented** | Reserved for an overall method whose human work is extended across several governed tasks. Define the augmentation and its limits when used. | Do not alternate casually with `AI-assisted`. |
| **model-neutral** | Describes guidance stated as durable responsibilities and tests rather than instructions tied to one product or model. | Model-neutral does not mean model differences are irrelevant to validation. |
| **release** | An authorized, versioned distribution of specified files or records to a named audience under declared rights, restrictions, and maintenance conditions. | Saving, exporting, uploading, and publishing are not interchangeable with release. |
| **version** | An identified state of an artifact, corpus, manuscript, resource, or release whose changes can be reconstructed. | A date alone may label a snapshot but is not always a sufficient version identifier. |
| **Working Edition** | The formal label for a public but unfinished book release. Capitalize only when used with a numbered edition, such as `Working Edition 0.2`. | Do not call a private build a Working Edition. |
| **companion material** | Maintained material outside the numbered book, including templates, instructor resources, implementation notes, extended examples, benchmarks, and tool guidance. | Companion material is part of the project but not part of the body-word target. |
| **tool card** | A dated companion record for a specific product or service, including use case, access conditions, data handling, limitations, validation status, and review date. | Tool cards do not carry stable methodological claims. |
| **maintenance policy** | The declared route for correction, update, preservation, deprecation, and retirement after release. | A change log alone is not a maintenance policy. |

## Capitalization and editorial conventions

1. Use sentence case for generic methodological terms: `evidence map`, `source family`, `human gate`, `historical claim`, `candidate record`, and `stopping rule`.
2. Use title case for formal Part and chapter titles, named resources, and exact interface or schema labels: `Part IV: Collection and Preparation`, `Historical Evidence Chain Register`, and `Working Edition 0.2`.
3. Capitalize formal lifecycle labels in diagrams, tables, and crosswalks: `Stage 1: Position`. Use lowercase in running prose: `during the position stage`.
4. Write `AI` in capitals, `generative AI` in sentence case, and `AI-assisted` or `AI-augmented` with a hyphen before a noun.
5. Write `lifecycle`, `dataset`, `database`, `codebook`, `workflow`, and `website` as single words.
6. Write `open access` as a noun and `open-access` before a noun. Write `oral history` as a noun and `oral-history` before a noun.
7. Hyphenate compound modifiers where needed: `source-to-claim chain`, `claim-dependency register`, `model-neutral guidance`, `rights-cleared document`, and `analysis-ready corpus`.
8. Use `fact-check` as a verb, `fact-checking` for the activity, and `fact-check log` for the artifact.
9. Use `Second Brain` only for the named knowledge-management concept or an exact project label. Prefer `research infrastructure`, `project memory`, or the specific artifact when making a methodological claim.
10. Preserve exact capitalization in quotations, archival titles, software names, and published schemas. Explain rather than silently normalize a source's terminology.

## Controlled replacements for structural revision

| Legacy or ambiguous wording | Canonical replacement |
|---|---|
| seven-stage architecture | seven-stage evidence-map pathway |
| six-stage literature lifecycle | six-stage literature pathway |
| PKM-AI research lifecycle or loop | PKM-AI pathway mapped to the research lifecycle |
| historical research cycle, when presented as a rival architecture | historical research pathway or historical-sources view of the research lifecycle |
| source type, when production process is meant | source family |
| source family, when format is meant | file type or media format |
| human-in-the-loop, when authority is meant | named human gate plus the decision and authorized role |
| AI-generated evidence | AI-produced proposal or derivative, unless the model output is itself the source under study |
| verified as true | verified against the named source or criterion |
| complete coverage | coverage adequate for the declared evidence design |
| final corpus | frozen or released corpus with a version identifier |
