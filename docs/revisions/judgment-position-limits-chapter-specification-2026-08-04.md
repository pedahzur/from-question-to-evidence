# Chapter Specification: Judgment, Position, and the Limits of Evidence

## Status and role

- **Project:** *From Question to Evidence: A Living Field Guide for Qualitative Research*
- **Part:** I, Judgment, Evidence, and AI
- **Planned position:** After “Origins and Purpose” and before “AI and Research Integrity”
- **Lifecycle function:** Stage 1, Position
- **Target length:** 2,500–3,500 words before notes
- **Principal artifact:** Epistemic-position statement
- **Running case:** Invented and composited municipal oral-history study; no real participant material
- **Status:** Approved drafting specification for WS2; not manuscript prose

## Chapter contract

### Governing claim

Evidence becomes reviewable when researchers document how position, relationships, categories, institutions, and access shape the inquiry. Such documentation exposes judgment to criticism. It does not make judgment neutral, complete, or harmless.

### Reader change

Before reading this chapter, a reader may treat reflexivity as a personal disclosure, a corpus as material found rather than produced, and an audit trail as a guarantee of objectivity. After the chapter, the reader should be able to state the inquiry's knowledge commitments, locate the researcher and other authorities within the evidence chain, distinguish coverage from representation, and identify what documentation cannot repair.

### What the chapter must establish

1. Every inquiry starts from a position, even when the researcher leaves that position unstated.
2. Sources become evidence only through a documented relation to a question and claim.
3. Researchers produce records, categories, relationships, exclusions, and interpretations. They do not merely govern material produced elsewhere.
4. Participants, communities, archives, institutions, and rights holders may possess authority that limits collection, interpretation, disclosure, or release.
5. Emergent and interpretive inquiry may revise its frame as meanings, relations, and absences become visible.
6. Coverage measures adequacy against a declared evidence design. It does not establish that the corpus represents a phenomenon or world in full.
7. Auditability permits criticism and reconstruction. It cannot remove power, settle interpretation, or convert situated judgment into neutrality.

### What the chapter must not claim

- Positionality can be discharged through a short autobiographical paragraph.
- Disclosure cancels bias or unequal power.
- One epistemology governs historical, interpretive, participatory, comparative, ethnographic, and case-oriented research.
- More records create a neutral or representative corpus.
- Community participation automatically equalizes authority.
- Transparency requires releasing protected, restricted, or harmful material.
- An audit trail proves that a conclusion is true.

## Argument spine

| Movement | Function | Planned words | Required consequence |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1. The same question, different evidence | Open with the synthetic municipal case. Two plausible access positions yield different encounters, records, absences, and risks. Label every detail as invented or composited. | 250–350 | The reader sees that evidence design begins before search. |
| 2. Knowledge starts somewhere | Define situated and partial knowledge in plain language. Show that questions, source categories, and standards of relevance arise from a position. | 400–500 | Position becomes a methodological input, not a confession. |
| 3. The researcher enters the evidence chain | Show how recruitment, rapport, interview questions, field notes, translation, coding, and archival selection produce records and categories. | 550–700 | Provenance expands to include relationships and researcher action. |
| 4. Authority is distributed and unequal | Address participant refusal, changing consent, community rules, gatekeepers, institutional power, archival formation, and damage-centered inquiry. | 500–650 | Authority and accountability can limit what the researcher may do. |
| 5. A frame may need to move | Explain emergent design, interpretive inquiry, contradiction, and reopening without presenting flexibility as undocumented improvisation. | 350–450 | Revision requires a reason, a prior version, and a human gate. |
| 6. Auditability has limits | Distinguish auditability from objectivity, transparency from indiscriminate disclosure, coverage from representation, and verification from truth. | 500–650 | The chapter's governing claim is tested rather than merely repeated. |

## Required distinctions

| Term | Required distinction |
|---|---|
| Position | The inquiry's stated knowledge commitments, authority structure, intended claim types, and limits |
| Positionality | The changing social, institutional, political, linguistic, and embodied relations through which the researcher is read and acts |
| Reflexivity | Repeated examination of how those relations and assumptions change research decisions and interpretations |
| Transparency | A variable practice of making specified information available to a named audience under ethical and legal limits |
| Auditability | The ability to reconstruct and question declared decisions, states, transformations, and dependencies |
| Coverage | Adequacy relative to the declared evidence design |
| Representation | A separate claim about how a corpus relates to a population, process, experience, or world |
| Authority | The legitimate power to permit, refuse, interpret, restrict, revise, or release specified research acts or objects |
| Accountability | The obligation to explain and answer for decisions and consequences |

## Epistemic-position statement

The chapter's durable artifact must contain these fields:

1. Project, version, author, date, and next review point.
2. Inquiry and intended answer form.
3. Knowledge sought and claims explicitly outside scope.
4. Methodological and interpretive commitments in plain language.
5. Researcher roles, relevant positions, competencies, dependencies, and conflicts.
6. Participant, community, archive, institutional, and rights-holder authority.
7. Relationships through which evidence will be discovered or produced.
8. Provisional categories, inherited labels, and contested terms.
9. Expected forms of unequal visibility, silence, refusal, and inaccessible evidence.
10. Intended source and encounter families and their production conditions.
11. Ethical, legal, political, linguistic, and technical limits.
12. Conditions that require reframing, consultation, abstention, or withdrawal.
13. Proposed AI role, prohibited delegations, and link to the AI pre-flight record.
14. Approval decision and named human gate.

The statement must be versioned. A change in the question, participant authority, source universe, claim type, researcher role, or release audience triggers review. Earlier versions remain part of the audit trail.

## Worked case requirements

The municipal oral-history fragment must contrast at least two evidence-producing relations, such as access through a municipal archive and access through tenant networks. It must include refusal, a contested institutional label, a translation or terminology decision, and one form of evidence that cannot be released. The case must show how these conditions alter the question frame and evidence design. It must not imply that the researcher can solve unequal authority through disclosure alone.

All participants, excerpts, field notes, permissions, and institutional details remain invented or composited. The case protocol must identify them as synthetic before the first excerpt.

## Exercise and completion signal

The reader completes a first epistemic-position statement for an active or proposed inquiry, then asks a peer to identify one hidden authority relation, one unexamined category, one likely silence, and one claim the evidence design cannot support.

The exercise is complete when the artifact names the intended claim type, the researcher and other authorities, the main evidence-producing relationships, at least one limit, at least one reopening trigger, and the decision maker who approves the position. A generic identity list does not pass.

## Integrity checkpoint

Before the reader proceeds, the chapter asks:

- Does the artifact expose a consequential research decision, or only describe the researcher?
- Does it identify authority held by participants, communities, archives, institutions, and rights holders?
- Does it record what may not be collected, inferred, disclosed, or released?
- Does it distinguish a search or access failure from absence in the world?
- Does it name who can require reframing, escalation, or abstention?

Any unresolved authority, consent, safety, or rights question becomes a stop condition for the affected work.

## Handoffs

- **Upstream:** the book contract and Part I opening establish the promise and audience.
- **Next chapter:** “AI and Research Integrity” applies the position to bounded AI use. The reader completes `templates/ai-preflight-record.md` before the first guided AI workflow.
- **Part II:** the approved position governs the question frame, categories, source families, coverage test, and stopping rule.
- **Part IV:** the artifact becomes an input to collection permissions, field relations, provenance, transformation, withdrawal, and corpus admission.
- **Part V:** analytic memos and claim audits record where position and relationship affect interpretation.
- **Part VI:** expression and release decisions return to the authority, limits, and audience stated here.

## Drafting acceptance test

The future chapter draft passes only when:

- its governing claim appears within the first ten percent;
- every section changes the argument and hands a consequence forward;
- the synthetic case is labeled and contains no implied real participant data;
- the researcher appears inside provenance where evidence is co-produced;
- every use of `coverage` names the evidence design being assessed;
- positionality is treated as dynamic practice rather than identity disclosure;
- auditability is defended without being equated with neutrality or unrestricted transparency;
- each external claim has a verified source and page-level locator;
- the completed and blank artifacts have been tested; and
- the chapter hands the reader directly to the AI pre-flight gate.
