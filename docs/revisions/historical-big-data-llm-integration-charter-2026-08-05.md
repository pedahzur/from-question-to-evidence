# Historical Big Data and LLM Integration Charter

## Decision

*From Question to Evidence* will be developed as an English-first and then Hebrew
full-cycle methods book for students and researchers conducting historically
grounded social research with large, heterogeneous source collections and large
language models.

This decision refines the approved six-Part architecture and ten-stage research
lifecycle. It does not create a second architecture. It makes explicit the
intellectual program that already connects the original textbook, the two
foundational papers, and the Historical Evidence Chain project.

## The three contributions being joined

| Contribution | Core problem | Durable contribution to the book |
|---|---|---|
| Structured and unstructured big data | Political research often analyzes what is easy to structure while losing context, source production, and causal sequence. | Treat large source collections as heterogeneous evidence whose form must match the question and claim. |
| Networked source management and PKM | Digital abundance creates a paradox of plenty: more searchable sources and less capacity to compare them in context. | Build a persistent, linked research memory that preserves source, annotation, decision, contradiction, and retrieval route. |
| Historical Evidence Chain | AI can collapse transcription, normalization, extraction, interpretation, and claim into one fluent output. | Separate each transformation, bind consequential observations to source passages, validate local records, and audit downstream claims. |

Together they support one argument: scale becomes useful only when a researcher
can move from source abundance to a bounded corpus and from that corpus to an
inspectable claim without severing context or responsibility.

## Binding lifecycle crosswalk

| Lifecycle stage | Big-data contribution | PKM contribution | Historical evidence-chain contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Position | State expertise, language limits, digital inequalities, and the consequences of source availability. | Record authority, dependencies, and persistent project context. | Name the institutions and relations that produced and preserved the record. |
| Frame | Begin with a theory-informed question rather than letting available data set the problem. | Preserve versions of the question and reasons for change. | Define the intended claim type and the source support it would require. |
| Design evidence | Distinguish structured and unstructured source families and specify what each can show. | Link concepts, terms, repositories, and source families without treating them as identical. | Plan witnesses, transcriptions, passages, observations, and claim dependencies. |
| Discover | Search large digitized and born-digital collections through multiple vocabularies and routes. | Save queries, failed searches, candidate notes, and cross-project connections. | Treat retrieval as selection and record archive, catalogue, OCR, and ranking limits. |
| Collect | Capture authorized sources and their metadata without mistaking availability for permission. | Maintain stable source identity and archival copies where lawful. | Record the source witness, holding institution, locator, access terms, and integrity information. |
| Prepare | Convert unstructured material through OCR, transcription, translation, normalization, and coding. | Preserve source and derivative as linked but distinct objects. | Bind each transformation to its input, version, uncertainty, and reviewer. |
| Analyze | Combine close reading, qualitative structuring, comparison, sequence, and selective counting. | Connect evidence, concepts, cases, memos, and codebook versions. | Keep source-stated content, normalization, inference, and document-specific roles separate. |
| Test claims | Use rivals, negative cases, chronology, dependence, and explicit causal tests. | Preserve contradictions, rejected explanations, and unresolved verification work. | Validate source-bound observations and audit historical claims through declared dependencies. |
| Express | Write from approved artifacts and return every consequential proposition to its support. | Recover prior decisions and source locators while drafting. | Preserve attribution, uncertainty, quotation fidelity, and the smallest defensible claim. |
| Release and maintain | Release only materials whose rights, errors, and limits are documented. | Version the manuscript, corpus, decisions, and corrections across languages. | Propagate a correction through every dependent observation and claim without erasing the original. |

## Revised audience and promise

The primary reader is a graduate student undertaking a historically grounded
qualitative or mixed evidence project. The reader may work with archives,
newspapers, interviews, administrative records, web collections, images, audio,
video, event data, or scholarly literature. No programming background is assumed.

The book promises a complete route from the first question to a maintained public
or restricted research object. Big data describe the scale, variety, velocity,
and source heterogeneity that make older habits difficult to sustain. LLMs enter
as bounded assistants for discovery, transformation, comparison, and challenge.
They do not inherit authority over corpus admission, interpretation, causal
judgment, ethics, or release.

## Book and companion boundary

The numbered book contains responsibilities, distinctions, worked cases, human
gates, and artifacts that should survive changes in software. Companion labs hold
versioned prompts, schemas, full codebooks, implementation specifications,
benchmarks, and tool cards. The historical lab is the first complete specialist
implementation under this boundary.

## Bilingual commitment

English remains the source edition while the architecture and terminology
stabilize. Hebrew is a governed parallel edition, not a one-time automated
translation. Stable chapter identifiers, a bilingual glossary, translation
status, right-to-left testing, citation parity, and source-version tracking are
required before a Hebrew chapter can be labeled current.

## Integration rules

1. Preserve the 2020 and 2022 papers as intellectual foundations and cite them at
   the points where their arguments do work.
2. Preserve the Historical Evidence Chain repository's source commit and license
   boundary.
3. Keep the ten-stage research lifecycle as the only book-level sequence.
4. Teach a no-code path first and place scripts and database implementations in
   advanced routes or companion material.
5. Use structured data when the research question requires structured comparison;
   use unstructured sources when context, meaning, process, or attribution is
   indispensable; combine them when the inferential task requires both.
6. Treat AI output as a candidate, derivative, measurement, comparison, or
   challenge until a named human gate changes its state.
7. Never infer historical absence from a failed digital search or evidentiary
   independence from repeated wording.
8. Keep public claims within the rights, uncertainty, and coverage of the released
   corpus.
