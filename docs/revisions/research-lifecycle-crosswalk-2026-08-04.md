# Research Lifecycle Crosswalk

## Status and authority

- **Project:** *From Question to Evidence: A Living Field Guide for Qualitative Research*
- **Record date:** 4 August 2026
- **Decision authority:** Ami Pedahzur
- **Status:** Binding G1 architecture record
- **Scope:** Existing and planned methodological pathways

This crosswalk establishes one reader-facing research lifecycle for the book. Domain pathways retain their own sequence where the work requires it, but they no longer function as competing architectures. A pathway may enter the lifecycle after Position, stop before Release and maintain, recur across several stages, or mark a stage as not in scope.

## The ten-stage research lifecycle

| Stage | Governing question | Minimum durable artifact | Human gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Position | What knowledge is sought, by whom, and under which commitments and authority relations? | Epistemic-position statement | Approve the claim type, responsibilities, authority, and limits |
| 2. Frame | What is the bounded or provisionally bounded inquiry? | Question frame | Approve scope, exclusions, answer form, and reopening conditions |
| 3. Design evidence | What concepts, cases, actors, encounters, and source families could bear on the question? | Concept-and-context grid and source-family map | Approve categories, sampling logic, and rival routes |
| 4. Discover | How will candidate evidence be found without confusing retrieval with coverage? | Search and discovery log | Approve search changes and candidate-promotion rules |
| 5. Collect | What may be gathered, from whom, and under which permissions? | Collection protocol and permission register | Approve access, consent, sampling, and collection |
| 6. Prepare | Which transformations make the material usable, and how will lineage be preserved? | Transformation and provenance log | Approve corrected derivatives and corpus admission |
| 7. Analyze | Which comparisons, codes, sequences, or interpretations answer the question? | Analytic memos, matrices, and codebook | Approve analytical changes and record disagreement |
| 8. Test claims | What supports, qualifies, contradicts, or cannot assess each claim? | Claim-dependency and disconfirmation register | Approve load-bearing claims, qualifications, and limits |
| 9. Express | How will the argument preserve evidence, attribution, uncertainty, and dissent? | Versioned manuscript and change record | Approve prose, citations, figures, and disclosures |
| 10. Release and maintain | What may be shared, withheld, corrected, updated, preserved, or retired? | Release manifest and maintenance policy | Approve release and later changes |

The sequence is ordered but recursive. A failed validation test may reopen preparation. A new archival term may reopen discovery. A participant's withdrawal may change the corpus, analysis, expression, and release decision. The lifecycle therefore records governed transitions rather than a one-way production line.

## Pathway crosswalk

### Evidence-map pathway

| Lifecycle stage | Pathway function and artifact | Scope note |
|---|---|---|
| Position | Uses the Part I epistemic-position statement as a governing input | Supporting dependency, not a separate pathway step |
| Frame | Step 1, Frame the Question; produces the question frame | Primary |
| Design evidence | Steps 2–3, Decompose the Question and Map Source Families; produce the concept-and-context grid and source-family inventory | Primary |
| Discover | Step 4, Search Iteratively; produces the search log and candidate register | Primary |
| Collect | Produces a collection plan and promotion criteria, but does not itself gather a corpus | Handoff only |
| Prepare | Not in scope | Begins in Part IV |
| Analyze | Not in scope | Begins in Part V |
| Test claims | Step 6 tests coverage of the declared evidence design, not substantive research claims | Limited |
| Express | Step 7 assembles the evidence map, gap analysis, and stopping rule | Method artifact, not final argument |
| Release and maintain | Freezes a version and records reopening triggers | Limited maintenance function |

The pathway remains the core of Part II. Its seven steps are a view over lifecycle stages 2–4, with a governed handoff to collection and a bounded coverage test.

### Historical-sources pathway

| Lifecycle stage | Pathway function and artifact | Scope note |

| Position | States archive formation, researcher position, authority, survival, silence, and permissible claim types | Primary |
| Frame | Defines the historical question, period, jurisdiction, actors, and procedural context | Primary |
| Design evidence | Maps record-producing processes, source witnesses, repositories, terminology, and rivals | Primary |
| Discover | Uses the discovery loop to search, compare, and propose candidate relations | Primary |
| Collect | Captures the source witness, locator, custody, rights, and diplomatic transcription rules | Primary |
| Prepare | Preserves diplomatic transcription, passage, normalized derivative, observation, and transformation history as separate objects | Primary |
| Analyze | Moves from source-bound observation to declared interpretation while preserving attribution, chronology, identity, and dependence | Primary |
| Test claims | Uses the validation loop to test provenance, coverage, transformations, source support, rivals, and limits | Primary |
| Express | States historical claims with visible dependencies and uncertainty | Primary |
| Release and maintain | Releases rights-cleared registers and preserves versions, restrictions, and audit records | Primary |

The pathway is a specialist method in Part IV with explicit links to claim testing in Part V. Its discovery and validation loops remain useful internal sequences, but neither replaces the research lifecycle.

### PKM-AI pathway

The seven operations, Bound, Capture, Distill, Connect, Compute, Verify, and Express, describe research infrastructure that can recur across the lifecycle. Durable continuity and provenance belong in Part VI. The full Readwise audit, installation guidance, product comparisons, and implementation details belong in companion material.

## AI integrity as a governing overlay

AI integrity is not an eleventh stage and is not a separate pathway. It supplies stage-specific controls.

| Lifecycle stage | Required AI control |
|---|---|
| Position | Name the researcher who holds authority, the necessity of AI assistance, prohibited delegations, and accountability |
| Frame | Record the intended AI role, data classification, limits, and conditions requiring escalation or abstention |
| Design evidence | Test whether categories, source routes, benchmark material, or model access reproduce systematic exclusion or exposure |
| Discover | Treat outputs as candidates; preserve queries, failures, routes, dates, and known retrieval limits |
| Collect | Confirm authorization, consent, rights, data minimization, access controls, and permitted processing environment |
| Prepare | Preserve the source and derivative separately; log system, settings, transformations, errors, and validation samples |
| Analyze | Benchmark bounded tasks, preserve researcher memos, reject false equivalence, and require human judgment for interpretation |
| Test claims | Verify consequential outputs against sources, inspect rivals and counterevidence, and record abstention and residual error |
| Express | Cite sources rather than AI output, verify quotations and claims, and disclose material assistance where required |
| Release and maintain | Recheck rights, privacy, confidentiality, disclosure, versions, drift, correction routes, and retirement conditions |

## G1 conclusion

The crosswalk shows that the research lifecycle subsumes every existing and planned pathway without erasing domain differences. The evidence-map and mirrored-newspaper pathways stop before full collection and analysis. The PKM-AI pathway supplies recurring infrastructure. Writing by voice governs a specialist composition route. The literature, review, historical, event-database, and oral-history pathways cover broader but distinct combinations of stages. AI integrity governs all ten stages without becoming a competing sequence.
