# Speechify

Speechify provides text-to-speech products that read documents and web text aloud.
This card records product-dependent facts. It is not an endorsement and should be
reviewed before use.

## Suitable role

Use a text-to-speech reader to prepare a diagnostic listening pass for public or
otherwise authorized writing. Listen for missing words, repetition, sentence
load, transitions, and translation awkwardness. Do not treat natural playback as
evidence that an argument, citation, or translation is correct.

## Current capabilities

As of the review date, the Reader product page advertises support for Word files,
PDFs, web pages, highlighting, playback-speed controls, multiple devices, and more
than 60 languages. Speechify's API documentation is a separate product surface. It
lists a smaller group of fully supported languages and identifies others,
including Arabic and Hebrew, as beta. Reader claims and API support should not be
treated as interchangeable.

## Privacy and processing

The privacy policy defines user content to include uploaded text and documents. It
states that content may be stored locally and synchronized to servers in the
United States, and describes uses connected to service provision and algorithm
improvement. Terms, settings, and product behavior may differ by application,
subscription, institution, and platform.

Before upload, verify the current privacy policy, retention and synchronization
settings, model-improvement terms, institutional approval, collaborator
permission, and the sensitivity class of the manuscript. Do not upload participant
material, restricted archival text, or confidential coauthored prose to a consumer
service without explicit authorization.

## Project benchmark

Prepare a short listening test in every working language. Include proper names,
transliterations, citations, abbreviations, numbers, and terms whose pronunciation
can change meaning. Record whether the voice hides punctuation problems, invents
stress, or mispronounces recurring terms. Use the result to choose the voice and
speed for each listening task.

## Alternatives

Built-in operating-system voices, browser accessibility readers, institutionally
approved services, and local text-to-speech engines may be preferable for
sensitive or offline work. A human collaborator remains necessary for substantive
translation review and for prose whose meaning depends on cultural or disciplinary
usage.
