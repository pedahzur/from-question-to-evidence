# Project Roadmap

## Project contract

*From Question to Evidence* is a long-form, open-access, versioned field guide for graduate qualitative-methods students and experienced researchers adapting established practice to large, multilingual, multimodal, or AI-assisted projects.

The book teaches researchers to build a documented and ethically governed chain from question to corpus, analysis, claim, public expression, and maintenance, while keeping AI assistance bounded, traceable, and subject to human judgment.

The planned body is approximately 75,000–90,000 words, excluding references and companion documentation. Stable method belongs in the numbered book. Dated tool guidance, installation instructions, product comparisons, benchmarks, extended implementations, and the Skills and Agents Lab belong in companion material.

## Binding architecture

The book has six Parts:

1. Judgment, Evidence, and AI
2. Designing the Evidence Base
3. Literature as Evidence
4. Collection and Preparation
5. Analysis and Claim Testing
6. Writing, Sharing, and Maintaining

Its ten-stage research lifecycle is Position, Frame, Design evidence, Discover, Collect, Prepare, Analyze, Test claims, Express, and Release and maintain. Domain sequences are pathways through these stages. The binding crosswalk is recorded in `docs/revisions/research-lifecycle-crosswalk-2026-08-04.md`.

The planned forty-five content units will be consolidated into approximately 32–36 numbered chapters. A numbered chapter must teach one principal competence, produce one principal artifact, or establish one necessary claim. Existing material will not dictate the architecture merely because it has already been drafted.

## Part I: Judgment, Evidence, and AI

**Purpose:** Establish the book's epistemic commitments, responsibility structure, limits, AI controls, and lifecycle before procedural work.

**Required work:**

- Write the chapter on judgment, position, power, reflexivity, archival formation, community authority, and the limits of auditability.
- Move AI integrity before the first guided AI workflow.
- Create an AI pre-flight gate covering authorization, data classification, necessity, rights, validation, logging, and escalation.
- Revise the opening and evidence-map overview so that coverage always names the declared evidence design rather than the world.
- Keep drifting product facts out of the stable chapter sequence.

**Principal artifacts:** epistemic-position statement and AI pre-flight record.

**Dependency and gate:** begins after G1; passes at G2 when a skeptical interpretive-methods reader can identify the book's commitments and no guided AI work precedes the integrity gate.

## Part II: Designing the Evidence Base

**Purpose:** Move from a research problem to a bounded, inspectable strategy for finding and evaluating potential evidence.

**Required work:**

- Retain the seven-stage evidence-map pathway.
- Thread the composite municipal oral-history case through framing, concept and context design, source families, discovery, coverage, gaps, and stopping.
- Preserve the mirrored-newspaper reconstruction as a case interlude on multilingual discovery, transmission, dependence, and failed searches.
- Clarify the handoff from an evidence map to actual collection.
- Add a completed evidence map, stopping rule, and reusable blank resources.

**Principal artifacts:** question frame, concept-and-context grid, source-family inventory, search log, evidence register, gap analysis, evidence map, and stopping rule.

**Dependency and gate:** oral-history case design follows G2 and passes at G3. Part II resources then become inputs to Part IV.

## Part III: Literature as Evidence

**Purpose:** Build, evaluate, and synthesize a bounded literature corpus without treating retrieval, repetition, or citation frequency as field-level knowledge.

**Required work:**

- Retain the six-stage literature pathway and map it to the research lifecycle.
- Separate literature discovery, corpus admission, comparison, synthesis, coverage testing, and release maintenance.
- Consolidate advanced review forms, heterogeneity, dependence, selective reporting, reproducibility, and living-review governance.
- Decide during integration whether meta-analysis remains one compact advanced chapter or a companion dossier.
- Move skills, agents, product implementation, and full project-context guidance out of the numbered review chapter.

**Principal artifacts:** review protocol, vocabulary map, search log, literature corpus register, synthesis matrix, coverage audit, and living-review policy where applicable.

**Dependency and gate:** the current pathway may be revised after G1; final specialist integration is tested at G7 and evidence quality at G8.

## Part IV: Collection and Preparation

**Purpose:** Turn a collection plan into a lawful, ethical, documented, and analysis-ready corpus.

**Required work:**

- Distinguish discovery, collection, encounter production, and corpus admission.
- Cover permission, consent, access, field relations, gatekeepers, sampling, and changing participant authority.
- Cover document, interview, observation, image, audio, video, web, and born-digital capture.
- Treat OCR, transcription, translation, anonymization, pseudonymization, normalization, redaction, and format conversion as logged transformations.
- Define storage, access, retention, withdrawal, deletion, preservation, and corpus handoff.
- Integrate historical sources and event databases as specialist methods using the shared lifecycle vocabulary.

**Principal artifacts:** collection protocol, permission and access register, capture log, transformation and verification log, restriction matrix, corpus manifest, and unresolved-items queue.

**Dependency and gate:** begins after the oral-history case passes G3; passes at G4 when every corpus object has documented origin, authority, transformation, sensitivity, and verification status.

## Part V: Analysis and Claim Testing

**Purpose:** Show how a documented corpus becomes an interpretation and how interpretations become bounded, reviewable claims.

**Required work:**

- Match analytical operations to question, evidence, case, unit, and claim type rather than software categories.
- Cover close reading, coding, memoing, comparison, sequence, narrative, discourse, process tracing, qualitative counting, and visualization.
- Preserve source voice, researcher inference, disagreement, rejected categories, missing perspectives, and non-assessability.
- Integrate rival explanations, negative cases, contradictions, dependence, uncertainty, and the smallest defensible revision.
- Relocate Bayesian updating from the event-database chapter to an advanced claim-testing method.

**Principal artifacts:** analytic-design memo, codebook, memo register, comparison matrix, sequence table, rival-explanation register, denominator sheet, and claim-dependency register.

**Dependency and gate:** begins after G4 and passes at G5 when every worked claim can be traced to corpus objects and analytical decisions.

## Part VI: Writing, Sharing, and Maintaining

**Purpose:** Carry accepted claims into prose and research objects without severing provenance or causing preventable harm.

**Required work:**

- Write from claim and evidence registers rather than unverified notes or model summaries.
- Govern quotation, paraphrase, translation, tables, figures, appendices, redaction, uncertainty, and dissent.
- Retain writing by voice as a specialist composition workflow.
- Define public, restricted, embargoed, and non-shareable objects.
- Add correction, version, citation, preservation, update, retirement, withdrawal, and deletion routes.
- Retain the durable project-memory argument from the PKM chapter and move product-specific material to companion resources.
- Replace the current future-facing conclusion with a substantive conclusion only after Parts IV–VI exist.

**Principal artifacts:** claim-to-paragraph outline, quotation and translation log, figure and table provenance checklist, versioned manuscript, release manifest, correction policy, and maintenance schedule.

**Dependency and gate:** begins after G5 and passes at G6 when the complete case reaches a safe release decision and every load-bearing paragraph remains traceable.

## Cross-cutting integration and assurance

After the six-part spine exists, the project will:

- consolidate or relocate specialist chapters and remove rival structural labels at G7;
- verify citations, facts, rights, resources, and drift-prone claims at G8;
- add observable learning objectives, completed examples, blank resources, rubrics, and instructor routes;
- test representative pathways with novice and experienced readers at G9;
- renumber files, complete accessibility and production checks, verify the live release, and preserve prior editions at G10.

No pilot claim will exceed the small tested group. No public release will be inferred from a local render, an internal candidate, or a repository push.

## Sequencing rules

The following work may proceed in parallel after G1:

- source discovery for Part I and the oral-history case;
- template prototyping and chapter outlining;
- citation verification for stable existing chapters;
- accessibility experiments on the current build;
- companion migration planning.

The following remains sequential:

- architecture before renumbering or major drafting;
- epistemic foundation before the oral-history case protocol;
- case protocol before collection examples;
- collection before analysis;
- analysis before writing from evidence;
- a complete representative pathway before reader testing;
- pilot revision before final copy edit and production;
- deployment before live verification.

## Release and companion boundary

- Working Edition 0.2 remains public, citable, and unchanged during the revision program.
- Internal milestone labels are workflow markers, not public editions.
- A revised public Working Edition requires a separate release decision by the active editorial authority after its declared gates pass.
- Version 1.0 requires G0–G10, a clean multi-format build, recorded accessibility inspection, a verified live site, release and rights manifests, preserved source and downloads, known limitations, and Ami's authorization.
- Prior public editions remain accessible and citable.

Companion material includes the Skills and Agents Lab, dated tool cards, installation and release instructions, product comparisons, the full Readwise and PKM audit, automation benchmarks, validation scripts, instructor materials, blank resources, completed specimens, and extended specialist implementations. Companion material may evolve more quickly than the numbered book, but it must still carry provenance, version, review-date, rights, and maintenance records.
