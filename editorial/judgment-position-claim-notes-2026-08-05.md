# Claim Notes: Judgment, Position, and the Limits of Evidence

## Verified claims and limits

| Cite key | Pages inspected | Claim supported | Limit or caution |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `haraway1988situated` | 579, 581, 583, 586, 590 | Accountable knowledge is embodied, located, partial, mediated, and answerable for how it sees. Critical positioning replaces a view from nowhere. | Does not supply a generic checklist for positionality statements or make every partial account equally adequate. |
| `england1994personal` | 82, 86–88 | Fieldwork is intersubjective; research relations may be reciprocal, asymmetrical, or exploitative; location affects questions, conduct, and writing; feedback and claims to give voice do not erase researcher control. | Does not establish that reflexive disclosure dissolves hierarchy. |
| `finlay2002swamp` | 212–15, 225–26 | Researchers participate in collection, selection, and interpretation. Reflexivity takes different forms and should fit epistemological commitments. Personal disclosure and methodological logs can aid interpretation and scrutiny. | Reflexivity can center the researcher, distract from evidence, or claim authority through candor. It is not an end in itself. |
| `soedirgo2020active` | 527–30 | Positionality is dynamic and contextual rather than a fixed insider or outsider status. Active reflexivity records expectations, examines how others read the researcher, involves other perspectives, and revises practice. | Does not promise that repeated reflection eliminates power or bias. |
| `tuck2009suspending` | 409–16, 422–24 | Damage-centered inquiry can reproduce one-dimensional accounts. A theory of change shapes evidence and audience. Desire-based framing holds complexity, and communities may refuse, redirect, or govern research. | Does not mean that researchers may substitute celebratory narratives for documented harm. |
| `trouillot1995silencing` | 24–26 | Power and silence enter historical production during fact creation, archival assembly, retrieval, and retrospective significance. Participants and nonprofessional actors shape narratives before the historian writes. | Not all silences are equivalent or recoverable through the same method. |
| `jacobs2021qtd` | 174, 177–83 | Qualitative traditions require different forms of evaluability. Ethics and protection can outweigh maximal sharing. Researchers can document access, evidence generation, analysis, position, risk, and changed plans without releasing all underlying material. | Does not endorse secrecy by default or define one universal transparency standard. |

## Drafting decision

The seven verified sources cover the chapter's current load-bearing claims about situated knowledge, active reflexivity, evidence production, unequal authority, damage-centered framing, archival silence, and ethically limited transparency. They do not close the planned decolonial source check. The chapter can enter internal review, but the source-plan stop rule and G2 remain open until the missing source is resolved or the source plan is formally amended with a justified substitute.
