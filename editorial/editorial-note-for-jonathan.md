# Note for Jonathan

Dear Jonathan,

I recently returned to the materials we assembled for the research-methods book about a decade ago. The archive was more substantial than I remembered. It contains long drafts on literature review and research preparation, shorter sections on field collection and post-production, cases drawn from terrorism data, and a large catalogue of software. It also contains duplicates, abandoned sections, copied articles, and product descriptions that now read as a history of the late 2010s.

The attached discussion draft does not restore that manuscript page by page. It recovers what I think was our strongest idea: researchers need a sequence for moving from an expansive question to a body of evidence whose composition and limits they can explain. The first organizing artifact is an evidence map. It links the question to concepts, source families, search decisions, evaluated records, gaps, and a stopping rule.

The expanded draft now contains a second complete module, *Literature as Evidence*. It moves from a review protocol and vocabulary map through multiple discovery routes, source- and claim-level evaluation, comparative reading, synthesis, coverage audit, and a literature stopping rule. Five new editable resources make the sequence usable rather than merely descriptive.

Generative AI enters as a research aid rather than the book's subject. The draft offers bounded uses for question decomposition, vocabulary expansion, missing-perspective checks, and gap analysis. It insists on verification, privacy, and an audit trail. This approach should remain useful when current products disappear.

I used our terrorism-data material as the running example because it shows how definitions, originating sources, collection systems, and coding rules produce different accounts of the same phenomenon. I avoided reproducing third-party PDFs, screenshots, and copied web material from the archive. The current draft uses only rewritten prose and verified source metadata.

The guide is designed for two audiences. Early-career researchers receive an explicit path and a concrete artifact at each stage. Experienced researchers receive methodological disputes, team protocols, and difficult cases. Both move through the same page pattern.

I would value your judgment on five questions. First, do the evidence-map and literature modules recover the central contribution we wanted the original book to make? Second, does the compact institutional-history example sufficiently widen the terrorism-data case? Third, does the literature module avoid becoming a disguised systematic-review manual while remaining rigorous? Fourth, how should we describe authorship and editorial responsibility if we continue together? Fifth, should the next module combine collection and post-production, as the new roadmap proposes?

The accompanying website treats the book as an open-access living field guide. Methodological chapters change through reviewed releases; templates and tool cards can change more often. English comes first, with Hebrew later. The public repository makes the work inspectable, but this remains a discussion draft rather than a formal launch.

Warmly,

Ami
