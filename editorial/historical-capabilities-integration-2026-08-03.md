## Editorial Decision

The material described in the request as `יכולות היסטוריונים` was identified with the recent *Historical Evidence Chain* project. Its distinct contribution is not another account of large-scale event extraction. It specifies how historical researchers preserve and test the transformations between an archival witness and a historical claim.

The manuscript now treats that contribution in a separate bridge chapter placed after *Building Event Databases with AI* and before *AI and Research Integrity*. This location creates a deliberate sequence. The event-database chapter explains scalable retrieval, verification, resolution, coding, and causal updating. The new chapter moves upstream to transcription and source-bound observations, then downstream to claim dependencies and audit.

## Material Adapted

The chapter adapts the evidence-chain model, the distinction among source-stated, normalized, and inferred content, document-specific roles, two-level validation, append-only correction, uncertainty recording, and the separation of Claims in Sources from Historical Claims. It also adapts the pilot's insistence that procedural support and historical correctness are different judgments.

## Material Not Imported

No Salem archival image, transcription, model output, empirical result, Airtable schema, REDCap schema, or full codebook was imported. The source project contains no completed empirical findings, and the chapter states that limitation. Platform-specific identifiers and controlled vocabularies remain in the companion project rather than becoming requirements for the general method.

## New Book Artifact

The accompanying `historical-evidence-chain-register.md` converts the stable method into an implementation-neutral worksheet. It preserves source witnesses, transcription versions, passages, observations, claims, audits, adjudications, and supersession history without duplicating the Salem instruments.
